# Simulation Reports

Three reports live here, all generated by the `sim` module. Don't edit them by hand.

- [`sim-latest.md`](sim-latest.md) covers single fights with the current content. Regenerate it after any rules or content change and commit it with that change. The simulator is deterministic, so the diff shows exactly what the change did.
- [`run-latest.md`](run-latest.md) covers whole runs of *The First Hour*, all three acts: how far runs get, the trajectory act by act, the bosses, what the bot chose, and which fights hurt. Regenerate it with the same rule.
- [`sim-baseline.md`](sim-baseline.md) is the first baseline: October 2026, the untuned numbers from docs 03 and 04. It stays frozen for comparison, so don't regenerate it.

```bash
./gradlew :sim:run --args="--seeds 200 --out reports/sim-latest.md"
./gradlew :sim:run --args="--runs 200 --out reports/run-latest.md"
```

To read one fight blow by blow, trace it by operative, deck, encounter and fight number:

```bash
./gradlew :sim:run -q --args="--trace oracle/starter/the_rent/3"
```

To play back a fight or a run from the web test client, copy its log there (**Copy log** or **Copy run code**) and pass the replay code. To watch one of the simulator's runs, trace it by operative and run number; it prints the run code too:

```bash
./gradlew :sim:run -q --args="--replay 'a1 oracle mid proxy_patrol 4242 S/// F/12,14// E'"
./gradlew :sim:run -q --args="--run-trace splinter/3"
```

## What the simulator does

- **Matrix:** every operative (Vowknight, Oracle, Splinter) with two decks fights every encounter.
  - The *starter* deck is the 10 cards from [docs/03](../docs/03-factions-and-operatives.md).
  - The *mid* deck adds 8 of that faction's sample cards.
- **Fights:** single combats at full HP and Paradox 0, with no relics, Imprints or rewards, and every enemy at its Act I stats (later acts scale their fights in runs). The bosses of Acts II and III are left out: they are built for a run's deck, level and Artifacts, so the run report covers them.
- **Bots:** all of them plan on copies of the state with the unseen part of the Future reshuffled and fresh dice, so none can peek.
  - `planner` searches the whole turn (a beam over sequences of plays), then scores each way of ending it by playing out the enemy phase and the next draw on a few copies. It answers Foresee with a heuristic order, except when an enemy is about to Erase or Blank the top of the Future: then it plans the rest of the turn after each candidate answer. It is the yardstick: the per-encounter tables and flags use it.
  - `greedy` looks one action ahead with the same evaluation, and ends the turn when nothing beats ending it.
  - `random` picks uniformly among legal actions. It is the floor.

## Baseline findings

The first baseline raised six findings. Their status after tuning pass 2:

| # | Finding | Status |

| 2 | The Vowknight is about twice as efficient as the others | **Narrowed.** The planner confirmed it is the design, not the bot. The [faction rework](#faction-rework-foresee-pays-elsewhere-acts) lifted the Oracle and the Splinter; the Vowknight is still ahead. |
| 3 | Most fights are too soft | **Tuned.** Every encounter averages inside its band, now measured with the planner. |
| 4 | Borrow is free on the last turn | **Fixed.** Debt carries into the next fight, and Borrow charges interest. Borrow use fell from 4.6–6.6 to 1.3–2.2 energy per fight. |

| 6 | Paradox and Unravel aren't exercised | **Partly.** Runs carry Paradox between fights, and the Splinter now Unravels in about one fight in twenty. See [the run layer](#the-run-layer-three-acts). |

## Content expansion: more cards, three new keywords

Runs repeated themselves. Each operative's rewards drew from 19–21 cards, so a run saw only 17–18 different cards in about 50 offers, and the bot took the same handful almost every run. This pass adds three keywords from [docs/04](../docs/04-combat.md#keywords) and 50 cards ([docs/03](../docs/03-factions-and-operatives.md)).

- **Martyr** (Order): Constants that answer the first HP you lose in each enemy phase. *Cilice* strikes back at the attacker, *Martyr's Vigil* and *Stigmata* give Plate and Might, and *Martyr's Crown* hits every enemy.
- **Accelerate** (universal): brings a Track item sooner, never below 1. With the Order's Scheduled blows (*Foretold Blow*, *Revelation*, *The Last Hour*) it makes a Prophecy deck.
- **Afterimage** (universal): the card repeats at half value, rounded down, at your next Dawn.
- **Cards.** The Order gets 14 new cards, the Convergence and the Errata 10 each, the neutral pool 10, and each era 2. Every card is within ±10% of its budget.
- **Rules.** A Martyr trigger counts as half a Dawn or Dusk trigger in the budget, and an Afterimage adds the value of its repeat. A Scheduled effect or an Afterimage whose target has fallen hits the weakest enemy instead.
- **Bot.** The planner now values a Scheduled effect a little less for each round it still waits, so Accelerate is worth something to it.

### What the expansion says

Measured with the same report code on the previous content (`main` before this pass) and on the new one, 200 runs per operative. The new [Card variety](run-latest.md#card-variety) section of the run report has the details.

| Operative | Pool | Different cards per run | Offers already seen that run | Runs that took the most-taken card |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Oracle | 21 → 47 | 17.3 → 28.3 | 64% → 39% | 96% → 76% |
| Splinter | 21 → 47 | 18.0 → 28.2 | 64% → 39% | 99% → 77% |
| Vowknight | 19 → 49 | 16.7 → 30.5 | 68% → 41% | 95% → 73% |

1. **A run sees about 65% more different cards**, and repeats fall from about two offers in three to two in five. Across 200 runs the bot takes 45–51 different cards: almost the whole pool.
2. **No card dominates as before.** The most-taken cards are new rares (*Ensemble Model*, *Multitude*, *The Last Hour*), taken in about three runs in four; before, the top card was in almost every run.
3. **Balance moved a little.** Run wins are 81% for the Oracle (78% before), 70% for the Splinter (77%) and 90% for the Vowknight (96%). Fights with the fixed starter and mid decks are unchanged. The run bot takes more cards now that more of them score well (decks end at 23–26 cards instead of 23–25) and buys fewer Artifacts (4.3–4.8 instead of 4.9–5.3), so its decks are less focused. A pickier player won't pay that. Rebalancing waits until the card pool is complete.

## Faction rework: Foresee pays, Elsewhere acts

The Oracle and the Splinter were reworked so that their factions' mechanics pay off in play ([docs/03](../docs/03-factions-and-operatives.md)).

- **The Oracle (Convergence): Foresee pays.**
  - The first Known card you play each turn costs 1 less.
  - Every starter card has a Calculated bonus instead of an Attuned one: +3 on Pulse and Firewall, +7 on Probability Lance.
  - Two new commons grow with the Known cards in your Present: *Expected Value* and *Confidence Interval*. *Deterministic Model* gains 2 Compute when Calculated.
  - The budget prices Calculated at ×0.5, like Attuned: Forecast shows 2 of the 5 cards you draw.
- **The Splinter (Errata): Elsewhere.** A card you Shift out of your Present acts as it goes.
  - The starters keep their old numbers and add a little to every Shift: *Splinter Cut* deals 3 to a random enemy, *Double Guard* gains 3 Block.
  - Two new commons, *Stet* and *Dele*, do as much sent away as played, or more.
  - The budget counts the better of the two uses in full and adds 0.15 of the other.

### What was tried first

- **Elsewhere at every Dusk.** In the first version, Elsewhere cards acted at each Dusk while they waited in the Other Hand. The Other Hand is never discarded, so three Elsewhere cards there were three free Constants, and the Splinter won 97–98% of runs (from 39%). Acting once, when Shifted away, ties Elsewhere to the Splinter's own verb and limits it to the Shifts you make.
- **Calculated alone wasn't enough.** Calculated starters and the new commons moved the Oracle only from 42% to 48% of runs, and it lost a little more HP per fight. Paying Foresee in energy, with the Known-card discount, took it to 73%. That is what the legendary experiment had shown: Foresee pays when it is paid in energy.
- **Weaker starters with bigger Elsewhere effects** (Deal 5 with Elsewhere 4–5; Gain 4 Block with Elsewhere 4) left the starter deck below its old level: 88–90% of fights won, 33–34% of HP lost. The bot spent every Shift on a trigger and lost the hand-fixing. Keeping the old numbers and adding a 3-point Elsewhere put the starter deck back where it was.

### What the rework says

1. **Both operatives now win about three runs in four**: the Oracle 78% and the Splinter 77%, up from 42% and 39%. The Vowknight wins 96%. The Act I boss now ends 1–3% of their runs (16–23% before).
2. **Starter decks hold their level, and the decks they grow into are stronger.** In won fights, the Oracle's starter deck loses 32% of its max HP (32% before) and its mid deck 31% (35%). The Splinter's starter loses 32% (31%) and its mid deck 22% (32%), close to the Vowknight's 20%. The Splinter's sample mid deck holds the whole Shift engine: *Borrowed Self* sends two cards away for 0 energy, *Elsewhen Guard* one more, and *Stet* and *Dele* are made for it.
3. **The bot Shifts every turn now**, often swapping two copies of the same card just to send one away (Double Guard ⇄ Double Guard). It plays *Stet* 0.03 times per fight and its four *Double Guards* 0.18 times in all: it Shifts them instead.
4. **The Tear counters the Oracle, and there is counterplay.** Ravels Erase the top card of your Future, which is where Forecast leaves the Known cards. Against the Fraying Cloister (two Ravels and a Loose End), the Oracle's starter deck at first won 19% of fights, down from 88% (and those wins already cost 81% of its HP). The counterplay is to put a card you can spare on top, or send the Foreseen cards to the bottom. Once the planner weighed those answers, it won 63%, and the Oracle's runs went from 73% to 78%. In runs, this threat-3 fight of Acts II and III is rare and deadly for every operative.
5. **The hard fights eased.** Before the rework, the Lattice Engine, the Brass Court, Capitano di Ventura and the Black Glass Sentinel were flagged *hard* for one or both starter decks. Now only the Black Glass Sentinel is, for the Splinter's starter deck: it wins 32% of those fights, up from 1%.
6. **The Vowknight is still the outlier**: 96% of runs, and 20% of HP lost per won fight.

## The run layer: three acts

[`run-latest.md`](run-latest.md) plays whole runs of all three acts of *The First Hour* ([docs/05](../docs/05-run-structure.md#the-first-hour-in-the-prototype)), 200 per operative.

- **The run bot.** It uses the planner in every fight. Between fights it follows simple heuristics:
  - On the Weft it weighs each Moment by type, threat and its HP.
  - It takes a card when one scores well on budget efficiency, rarity and deck shape.
  - At the Antiquarian it Erases a basic card, Inscribes, then buys.
  - At Still Points it heals below 60% HP, Steadies at Paradox 4 or more, and Inscribes otherwise.
  - It never Glimpses. It reads Omens only to look at an Elite.
  - It accepts every Anchor a Shrine offers, and always Rewinds when it falls with its Anchor unspent. It never Rewinds otherwise.
  - It picks Imprints from a fixed taste list (each operative's own engine first), and boss Artifacts by how well they fit the operative.
  - It is a baseline for run-level balance, not a strong player.

### Three acts: what changed to get here

- **Acts II and III joined the run**, with XP and Imprints, boss rewards and boss Artifacts, Standing effects and the Nexus. Later acts scale their fights: 125% HP and Might 1 in Milan, 150% HP and Might 1 in the Salt Waste.
- **The Automa Cavaliere**, first written from docs/03 at 150 HP with a 28-damage Joust, beat the Oracle in 47 of 48 fights and the Splinter in 40 of 47. It is now 120 HP with a 22-damage Joust, and its hearts hit a little softer.
- **The Nexus champions** were cut from 200/180/170 HP to 160/150/130, with smaller signature moves. The Thousandfold's Weak on every Guard kept the Oracle permanently Weak; now only her Chorus applies it.
- **Capitano di Ventura** first summoned a Crossbowman every other round, and the summons piled up (10 deaths in 18 fights). It now fights beside the one it brings.
- **A pair of Horse Archers** moved from threat 1 to threat 2. With Act III's scaling their 4×3 volley took 35% of max HP.
- **Boss card rewards.** Each faction has only one or two rares, so a first version filled the third slot with the faction's legendary. That put *Proof of MERIDIAN* in almost every Oracle's hand and doubled its win rate. Legendaries now come only from the 15% roll, and thin slots fill with uncommons.

### What three acts say

These findings predate the [faction rework](#faction-rework-foresee-pays-elsewhere-acts).

1. **Each act's boss is the gate, and it gates evenly.** Of the runs that reach it, the Oracle and the Splinter get past the Lattice Engine 77–83% of the time, the Automa Cavaliere 72–77%, and the Nexus champion 68–75%. They win **42%** and **39%** of whole runs; the Vowknight wins **96%**.
2. **Normal fights keep their bands in every act.** The deck grows about as fast as the scaling: threat-1 fights cost 8–12% of max HP in every act, threat 2 costs 19–29%, Elites 28–46%. Almost every death is at a boss.
3. **The trajectory.** Runs enter Act II at level 4 with a 15-card deck and 2 Artifacts, and Act III at level 5–6 with 19–21 cards and 3.5–4 Artifacts, always fully healed. Every boss is met at about 70% HP.
4. **The operative gap grows with the run.** The Vowknight kills the Cavaliere in 5.5–5.7 rounds for a third of its HP; the others need 6–7 rounds and half their HP. The gap is the same as in Act I, compounded three times.
5. **What helps the weak operatives most is their own engine.** The bot's Oracle takes Deep Forecast, Precomputed and Bayesian Prior almost every run, and the MERIDIAN Shard when it's offered. The legendary experiment above says Foresee pays when it is paid in energy.
6. **Rewind is used once per run by the Oracle and the Splinter** (each act brings a new Anchor). About a quarter of those runs still win.
7. **Which Cavaliere you meet matters.** MERIDIAN's escapement is the hardest heart for the Oracle (35% of fights won against 66% for the empty chest), partly because one of its Ripples gives Convergence enemies Might 1.

### Act I: what changed to get there

- **The Lattice Engine**, first written from docs/03:
  - At 120 HP, with a 6-per-Subroutine Cascade and Might rising every round, the Oracle and the Splinter won only 15–21% of boss fights.
  - It is now 85 HP. Its Subroutines are Bulwark (Guard 4), Lance (Attack 3), Jacquard (Glitch 1), then Overclock, so the Might ramp starts last. Its predictive guard is 6, its open attack 10, and the Cascade 5 per Subroutine.
- **Threat-3 fights** shrank to two strong enemies, or a Ravel with two Loose Ends. With three full enemies they took 60–80% of max HP from the weaker operatives.
- **The run bot** stopped picking up a second copy of a Constant. Two *Paradox Engines* on top of Paradox carried from earlier fights Unravelled the Splinter every other round.
- **A bug the tests caught.** Dealing a step could lose its own changes: the Frayed Tear Moments, and the shop's random draws.
- **Time travel (second pass).** Returning Moments, Anchors and Rewind joined the run. Their tests caught two more bugs:
  - Fights entered from the map paid 1% of their Hours.
  - Steady could create a fresh Anchor after one was spent, which allowed up to three Rewinds per act.

### Act I: what the runs said

1. **The operative gap carries into runs.** With the same bot, the Vowknight clears Act I in 100% of runs, the Oracle in 74% and the Splinter in 72%. Without Rewind they were at 58% and 48%.
   - The Vowknight kills the Lattice Engine in about 5 rounds; the others need 9–11, and the boss scales with time.
   - With mid decks at full HP, the Oracle beats the boss 93% of the time and the Splinter 66%. With starter decks, 14% and 10%: an act boss should beat a starter deck.
   - Rebalancing the operatives is parked by decision. When it resumes, these two reports measure it.
2. **Rewind is the Oracle's and the Splinter's safety net.**
   - 41% of Oracle runs and 51% of Splinter runs fall once and Rewind. About 40% of those go on to clear the act.
   - Most falls happen at the boss. The Anchor is usually still at step 1, so the Rewind replays the whole act at +5 Paradox.
   - Echoes rarely get fought (0.1 per run). A Rewind at the boss leaves no later step for the Echo, and the bot treats an Echo like any Elite.
   - The bot never Rewinds by choice. A player who Rewinds out of a bad Elite will use it differently, so these numbers say little about that use yet.
3. **Paradox is now a run resource.** The Splinter enters the boss at 2.7 Paradox on average and peaks at 6.1 per run (a Rewind from death adds 5). The others stay near 1, or near 3 for an Oracle who Rewinds.
4. **Returning Moments are common.** About 5–6 come back per run and the bot takes one of them. Fights return as Ambushes, which the bot dislikes, so it takes them less.
5. **Threat tiers read as intended:**
   - threat-1 openers cost 7–9% of max HP (the fight report flags them *trivial*, which is their job)
   - threat 2 costs 21–31%
   - threat 3 costs 25–38%
   - Elites cost 34–50%
6. **The bot is the weak link between fights.** It takes only 4–6 cards, 0.3–0.4 Elites and about 1.5 Inscribes per run. A human will build stronger decks, so read the clear rates as a floor.
7. **Unwrite is real counterplay.** Deleting an install keeps the Lattice Engine a Subroutine short, and the bot already does it.

## Tuning pass 2

### What changed

- **Vow of the Sword is back at +4**, by decision. The budget exception from pass 1 is gone.
- **Borrow interest.** The first Borrow of a turn costs 1 extra Debt: Borrow 1 and owe 2, Borrow 2 and owe 3 ([docs/04](../docs/04-combat.md#reservoir-and-borrow)).
- **The planner bot** became the yardstick. With interest, the economy is tighter, so the bands were re-checked with it. Only The Rent fell out of its band; its charged attack went from 16 to 20.
- **Bot fixes.** Known cards are valued with their Calculated bonuses. Strike buffs count only for an operative that Strikes: the planner had installed a bled-in *Gnomon Blade* as the Splinter.

### What pass 2 found

1. **The Vowknight's lead is real.**
   - The planner loses 15–16% of max HP per fight with the Vowknight, and 29–32% with the Oracle and the Splinter. The greedy bot showed the same gap.
   - A planner with twice the search width and nearly three times the samples changes nothing (15% against 26–30%), so the gap is not a search artifact.
   - It is mostly damage. The Vowknight wins in 3.0–3.6 rounds; the others take 3.9–5.2. Its free Strike adds 4–8 damage a turn, while Forecast and Shift add none.
   - **Experiment** (planner, 60 seeds, not applied):
     - Forecast that also draws 1 barely helps the Oracle: 30–32% → 29–30%.
     - Shift that makes the incoming card cost 1 less helps the Splinter more: 29% → 20–25%.
   - **Options:** buff Forecast and Shift along those lines, or accept the Vowknight as the easy operative (the docs call it the most forgiving) and tune encounters against the other two.
   - The only flags left are the Vowknight's: Condottieri, Plague Season and Glass Desert are trivial for it, and the Inquisitor is soft (18%).
2. **Borrow is a decision again.** With interest, the planner borrows 1.3–2.2 energy per fight and carries 0.5–0.9 Debt out of a won fight. Random play, which borrows freely, now loses up to half its fights.
3. **The planner uses the Track more:** the Oracle's mid deck Delays 0.49 intents per fight, and *Hold the Hour* is played 0.33 times per fight. Still rarely played (under 0.15 per copy per fight):
   - *Deterministic Model*, *Predictive Shield*, *Split the Moment*, *Elsewhen Guard*
   - *Hold Fast*, *Oathkeeper's Stand*, *Litany of Steel*, *Shield of the Line*

   Most are Skills that compete with the Vowknight's Vow or with racing. They are worth a design look before more bot work.
4. **Unchanged:** no single fight reaches Paradox 10.

## Tuning pass 1 (history)

These are the pass 1 numbers, measured with the greedy bot before Borrow interest existed.

### What changed

**Rules** ([docs/04](../docs/04-combat.md)):

- Blank cards are restored when your Past is reshuffled into your Future.
- Debt still owed when a combat ends is repaid at the first Dawn of your next combat.
- *Vow of the Sword* gives your Strike +2 instead of +4. That puts it far below its budget, so it is recorded as a reviewed exception (⚑ in [docs/03](../docs/03-factions-and-operatives.md)).

**Enemies.** Four simulation passes, aiming at 15–25% of max HP lost per normal fight and 35–50% per elite (greedy bot, averaged over the six operative and deck pairs). The new numbers are in the [docs/03 enemy rosters](../docs/03-factions-and-operatives.md#enemy-rosters). The main moves:

- Era natives and faction squads hit about 20–30% harder.
- Tear enemies gained small attacks, so a Tear fight always moves.
- The Drone Swarm is four drones at 10 HP and Attack 5. Three weak drones were cheap to block.
- Glass Desert pairs the Sandglass Golem with a Mirage. One slow charge on its own was easy to block.
- **Pruner:** a jab every round.
- **The Rent:** 82 HP, with attacks in its loop.
- **Inquisitor:** 60 HP, so its Fixed Purge lands even against fast decks.

**Bot:**

- The greedy bot values a Vow without its budget credit, since it pays for the restriction in play.
- It counts Debt at a win as a cost.

### What the numbers say now

1. **Fights land in their bands, and skill matters.**
   - Every encounter averages inside its band for the greedy bot.
   - Random play now loses 13–25% of its fights with the Oracle and the Splinter. The greedy bot loses none.
2. **The Vowknight is still the easiest operative by a wide margin.**
   - Its starter deck loses 15% of max HP per fight. The Oracle and Splinter lose 24–28%.
   - *Vow of the Sword* at +2 helped only a little: under the same enemies, +4 gives 13%.
   - A test with Strike at 3 instead of 4 barely moved it either.
   - The likeliest cause is the bot. One action of lookahead plays direct damage almost perfectly, but it can't plan a Forecast turn or a Shift.
   - **Next:** build the two-ply or MCTS bot from [docs/09](../docs/09-tech-architecture.md#testing-and-qa) before cutting the Vowknight further.
   - Two flags remain, both for the Vowknight's starter deck: Plague Season is trivial (7%) and the Inquisitor soft (12%).
3. **Borrow is now an every-turn habit.**
   - Fights are longer, and the bot borrows 1.1–1.4 energy per round: 4.6–6.6 per fight.
   - About 0.6–0.7 Debt per won fight now carries into the next fight.
   - So rolling Debt forward is still worth a free +2 energy once per run, where it used to be once per fight.
   - **Proposal:** if Borrow should be a real decision, the next lever is interest. Borrow 2, owe 3.
4. **Unchanged:**
   - Delay and draw cards still look weak to the bot (0–0.26 intent Delays per fight).
   - No single fight reaches Paradox 10.
